# Nested grouped cross-validation

Generated by `python scripts/nested_cv.py`. 4 classes (calm, happy, fearful, disgust), all 24 actors, 768 clips.

**Protocol.** Outer `GroupKFold(6)`: each fold tests 4 actors. Inside each fold, `GroupKFold(5)` over the remaining 20 actors chooses among 20 deployable configurations (2 poolings × 5 classifiers × {none, neutral-clip} speaker normalisation). The chosen configuration is refit on the 20 actors and scored on the 4. The baseline is fixed in advance and scored on the same folds. Stochastic models use 5 seeds; logistic regression and histogram gradient boosting (early stopping is off below 10,000 samples) give identical results for every seed.

## Overall (every actor tested once)

| Procedure | Accuracy, mean ± std over seeds |
|---|---:|
| Baseline (MLP / mean / no normalisation) | **60.55%** ± 0.73% |
| Nested selection (search, then test) | **69.27%** ± 0.00% |

## Is the gain real?

- **Speaker-level bootstrap, 95% interval for the gain** (24 speakers, 5000 resamples, averaged over seeds): [+4.1, +13.7] points.
- **Speakers improved / worse / unchanged:** 15 / 8 / 1 (sign test p = 0.2100; Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-speaker gains p = 0.0059).
- **McNemar per seed** (treats the 768 clips as independent, which they are not (each speaker contributes 32), so these p-values are optimistic): seed 0: b=97, c=162, p=0.0001; seed 1: b=92, c=152, p=0.0001; seed 2: b=91, c=154, p=0.0001; seed 3: b=86, c=158, p=0.0000; seed 4: b=85, c=160, p=0.0000.
